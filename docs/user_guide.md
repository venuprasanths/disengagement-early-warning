# Educator & Counselor User Guide

A practical handbook for academic counselors, advisors, and classroom teachers on how to use the Disengagement Early-Warning System responsibly and effectively.

---

## 1. What the Disengagement Risk Score Means (and What it Does NOT Mean)

### What the Score IS:
* **An Early Smoke Detector**: A mathematical fusion of five behavioral indicators signaling that a student's engagement trajectory is drifting away from healthy learning patterns.
* **A Resource-Allocation Guide**: A way for busy counselors (who may have 250+ students on their caseload) to prioritize who might benefit from a friendly proactive check-in this week.
* **A Multi-Signal View**: An objective look across in-seat presence, online effort, assessment velocity, help-seeking, and pulse feedback.

### What the Score is NOT:
* ❌ **NOT an Academic Diagnosis**: The model cannot diagnose ADHD, depression, cognitive disability, or family distress.
* ❌ **NOT an Intelligence or Ability Measure**: A student with a high risk score may be exceptionally bright but currently alienated, bored, or facing an acute crisis.
* ❌ **NOT a Disciplinary Instrument**: Under school policy, this score can **never** be used to assign detention, deduct grades, revoke privileges, or justify punitive measures.
* ❌ **NOT Infallible**: The score carries uncertainty intervals. A wide interval means the system has sparse data and advises caution.

## 2. Daily & Weekly Operational Workflow

### Step 1: Monday Morning Caseload Scan (Counselor View)
1. Log in to the **Stakeholder Trade-off Dashboard**.
2. Set the **Decision Threshold Slider** according to current counselor outreach capacity:
   - *Example*: If your team has capacity for 15 check-ins this week, calibrate the threshold so the flagged list matches your capacity (typically $\tau \approx 0.55\text{--}0.65$).
3. Sort the cohort by **Risk Score (High to Low)**.

### Step 2: Individual Profile Triaging
Before contacting any student, open their **Student Detail Card**:
1. **Check Uncertainty Interval**:
   - If marked **"Low Confidence (Wide Interval)"** (e.g. transfer students or new enrollments), DO NOT assume disengagement. The student simply lacks historical data.
2. **Review the 5-Family Contribution Breakdown**:
   - *If Help-Seeking is the primary driver*: Student is struggling with coursework but not attending office hours or tutoring. (Action: Pair with a peer tutor or create a low-stakes study circle).
   - *If Activity is the primary driver*: Student is logging in but spending negligible time reading or submitting late. (Action: Check time management, workload balance, or device access).
   - *If Assessment Trend is negative despite high attendance*: Student is "quietly struggling"—they are showing up diligently but failing to grasp compound concepts.
3. **Check Qualitative Notes**:
   - Look for recent teacher notes mentioning family bereavement, health issues, or athletic commitments.

### Step 3: Proactive, Affirmative Outreach
When reaching out to a student:
* **The "Curiosity, Not Surveillance" Rule**:
  - ❌ *Never say*: "Our early warning algorithm flagged you as high-risk for dropping out."
  - ✅ *Always say*: "Hi Maya, I'm checking in with students mid-semester to see how things are going. I noticed Physics has gotten heavy this month—how are you feeling about the recent labs?"
* **Action-Oriented Support**:
  - Offer tangible scaffolding: tutoring schedule, office hours walk-in times, study groups, or counselor chat.

## 3. Classroom Teacher Checklist

1. **Weekly Pulse Surveys**: Remind students to complete the 30-second weekly pulse survey on Fridays. Emphasize that their responses help faculty adjust course pacing.
2. **Qualitative Notes**: When you observe a subtle change in student demeanor (e.g., usually vocal student sitting silently in the back for 2 weeks), file a brief, objective teacher note. This provides human context that tempers algorithmic interpretation.
3. **Celebration of Recovery**: Notice students flagged as "Genuinely Improving"—acknowledge their effort when they attend tutoring or improve quiz scores.
